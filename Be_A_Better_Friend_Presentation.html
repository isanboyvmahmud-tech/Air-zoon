Section 01

Our Campaign Mission

The purpose of our friendship initiative and our 5 core pillars
“Our campaign encourages students to build stronger friendships through kindness, trust, honesty, respect, and support. A good friendship is not about being perfect; it is about being there for each other, especially when life becomes difficult.”
01. Kindness
(Mehribonlik)

Small everyday actions and warm words that help every classmate feel respected and appreciated.

02. Trust
(Ishonch)

Keeping private secrets confidential and proving to be someone your friends can always rely on.

03. Honesty
(Haqiqatgo'ylik)

Speaking the truth gently without spreading gossip, unnecessary drama, or rumors.

04. Respect
(Hurmat)

Respecting individual differences, personal boundaries, varied viewpoints, and emotions.

05. Support
(Qo'llab-quvvatlash)

Standing by your classmates during stressful exams, emotional hurdles, and difficult times.

Section 02

Five Golden Rules of Friendship

Essential principles every true friend should practice daily
1

Listen carefully

Give your friend time to speak. Do not interrupt or judge too quickly.

2

Be honest and trustworthy

Tell the truth and keep private information confidential.

3

Show respect

Respect different opinions, interests, boundaries, and feelings.

4

Offer support

If your friend has a problem, ask how you can help instead of ignoring them.

5

Solve problems calmly

Talk about misunderstandings politely. Apologise when you are wrong and forgive when you can.

Section 03

Three Pieces of Practical Advice

Realistic advice demonstrating empathy, integrity, and active inclusion
Active Listening

When a Friend is Sad

“If your friend feels sad, listen to them and ask what they need.”

Do not rush to offer unasked advice. Often, your quiet presence and active listening are the best comfort.

✓ Grammar: First Conditional (If + Present)
Integrity & Directness

Dealing with Conflict

“You should talk honestly instead of spreading rumours or making assumptions.”

Gossip destroys friendships. Direct and respectful one-on-one conversation resolves tensions quickly.

✓ Grammar: Modal Verb (should)
Warm Inclusion

Welcoming the Lonely

“You could invite a lonely classmate to join your group and help them feel welcome.”

Nobody deserves to feel isolated. An invitation to lunch or group study makes our school a friendlier place.

✓ Grammar: Modal Verb (could)
Section 04

Short Campaign Speech (4 Speakers)

Structured speaking roles ensuring equal participation for all members
Speaker 1 • Introduction Opening Lead

“Good morning, everyone! Today, we are presenting our campaign, ‘Be a Better Friend!’ Friendship makes our lives happier and our challenges easier. However, strong friendships need time, trust, and effort. Our slogan is: ‘Be the friend you wish to have!’”

Speaker 2 • The Five Rules Rules & Examples

“A good friend listens carefully, tells the truth, shows respect, offers support, and solves problems calmly. For example, listening is better than interrupting, and honest communication is stronger than gossip. We should respect our friends even when we have different opinions.”

Speaker 3 • Advice & Grammar Language Focus

“Here are three simple tips. If your friend feels sad, listen without judging. You should speak honestly and never spread rumours. You could also invite someone who feels left out to join you. If we support one another, our classroom will become a kinder place.”

Speaker 4 • Call to Action Conclusion & Next Steps

“Let’s take a friendship challenge! This week, do one kind thing every day: help someone, give a sincere compliment, or include a classmate in an activity. Small actions can create stronger friendships. Remember, a real friend does not have to solve every problem; sometimes, being present is enough. Be kind, be honest, and be a better friend!”

Section 05

Language Features & Grammar Focus

Grade 11 curriculum targets demonstrated in our presentation

1. Comparatives

Used to contrast positive behaviors with harmful attitudes:

• “Listening is better than interrupting.”
• “Honest communication is stronger than gossip.”
• “We can make a bigger difference together than alone.”

2. Modal Verbs

Used for giving advice, suggestions, and necessity:

• “You should speak honestly instead of spreading rumours.”
• “You could invite a lonely classmate to join your group.”
• “A real friend does not have to solve every problem.”

3. Conditionals

Zero & First Conditionals for cause and effect:

• “If your friend feels sad, listen without judging.”
• “If we support one another, our classroom will become a kinder place.”
• “If both friends communicate honestly, their friendship can become stronger.”

4. Linking Words

Connecting thoughts smoothly across sentences:

• Contrast: however, instead of, even when
• Exemplification: for example, such as
• Cause & Conclusion: because, also, together, finally

Section 07

Questions and Answers (Q&A)

Ready responses for teacher evaluations and peer inquiries
Q: What is the most important quality in a friend?
A: I think trust is one of the most important qualities because friends need to feel safe and respected.

Key idea: Trust forms the foundation of all genuine emotional connections.

Q: What should you do if you disagree with a friend?
A: We should stay calm, listen to each other, and explain our opinions politely.

Key idea: Differences of opinion should be celebrated, not fought over.

Q: How can you help a lonely student?
A: I could start a conversation, invite them to join an activity, and make them feel welcome.

Key idea: It only takes one welcoming person to erase loneliness.

Q: Can a friendship survive a misunderstanding?
A: Yes. If both friends communicate honestly and are willing to apologise, their friendship can become stronger.

Key idea: Overcoming challenges together cements trust.
